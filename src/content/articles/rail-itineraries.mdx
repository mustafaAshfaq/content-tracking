Rail itineraries fail for one of two reasons: connection windows that assume
everything runs on time, or itineraries built leg-by-leg without ever looking
at the whole day.

Start from the transfers, not the destinations. A connection under thirty
minutes should be treated as fragile unless you've verified the platform
distance in person or via station maps. Build in one buffer day every three
to four travel days — not for sightseeing, but as a release valve if a leg
runs late.

Booking flexibility matters more than price on the legs with tight
connections; paying slightly more for a re-bookable ticket on those specific
legs is usually the better trade.
