An index fund is a basket of stocks or bonds that tracks a market index
rather than a manager's picks. The pitch isn't that it's exciting — it's that
it's cheap and it doesn't rely on anyone guessing right.

The expense ratio is the number that matters most: a fund charging 0.03% a
year keeps almost all of your returns, while one charging 1% quietly
compounds a meaningful chunk away over decades. Two funds that hold nearly
identical stocks can have wildly different long-term outcomes purely because
of that fee gap.

Broad market index funds also remove a specific kind of decision fatigue —
there's no picking winners, just staying invested and reinvesting dividends.
